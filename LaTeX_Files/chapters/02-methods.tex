Image processing is a highly relevant procedure for deploying statistical pipelines. Without a solid and robust preprocessing the downstream task will not produce any relevant information. For this project we gathered pre-procecced sMRI images.
The fully description of the pre-processing steps can be investigated in J. Menzes et al. \cite{MENEZES2026111143}. Here we are going to explain the pre-processing in a short version.
 
One of the methods of preprocessing in MRI images is the bias field correction. The bias fields is a 
low-frequency spatially varying MRI artifact resulting from spatial inhomogeneity of the magnetic field, variations in the sensitivity of the reception coil, and the interaction between the magnetic field and the human body.
The bias fiels is dependent on the strength of the magnetic filed. By increasing the magnetic field, which increases the spatial resolution also increases the bias field, resulting in a higher Signal to noise ration. 
\\
Followed by the Image registration. This step is fundamental for further analysis. It aligns all images, in space positioning and inter-subject anatomically variability.   
\\
another important pre-processing method is the brain extraction, skull-stripping. Image contrast from non-brain tissue such as fat, skull, or neck can cause issues with downstream
analysis starting with the brain tissue segmentation. 
The brain extraction generates a mask that identifies brain voxels comprising gray-matter, white-matter, and Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of the cerebral sorte and subcortical structures, 
including the brain stemm and the cerebellum.

\subsection{Foundation model DINOv2}
\label{DINO}
DINOv2 is an advanced self-superviced learning technique, released 2023 by MetaAI. This foundation model is particular powerful in the fields of computer vision. 
It has a high accuracy in identifying including objects within iamges and video frames.
The model extracts the features directly from the images, and does not rely on text descriptions.
The architecture of the model is decided into 2 levels. 
The first level is the Image-level objective. 
The initial DINO architecture, consists of a student-teacher networks, that includes a larger network, the teacher, and smaller network, the student. 
Both of the network crop parts of the image, and feed that to the feature encoder. The feature encoder, will produce
embeddings, which will be further used. The student network is build by training on the embeddings. 
and the Teacher network is build by taking advantage of the weights of different student models.
The second level design is the pacth-level design. The vision trasformer devides the image into 4x4 batches, unrolls the array and
randomly masks the some of the input patches, that student network trains on. Principal component analysis (PCAs) are computed for patch features extracted by the network.
